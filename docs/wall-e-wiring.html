Wall-E Wiring Sheet

Seven servos, four controls, an LED strip and one battery. The servos, the switches and the LED strip plug into the servo headers on the Waveshare board, with the Pico riding on top. The pot uses the Pico's own broken-out pins.

Wiring rules and bench notes

  1. Header mapping works as labeled. Servos on GP0–GP6, the strip on GP7 and the controls on GP13–GP15 all respond on the pins the code names. If a header is ever moved, change src/common/pins.py, not the wiring.
  2. Controls use S and G only. The button and both toggles go between S and G on their header. Never use V: it is 5 V, and a switch across V and G shorts the servo rail.
  3. The pot's black wire is the signal. Black (middle pin) to GP26, red to 3V3, yellow to GND. Wired by color the other way, the reading sits near zero for most of the turn. Take 3V3 and GND from the Pico's own pins, never from a servo header.
  4. LED strip. Go by the pad labels on the strip, not the wire colors: 5V to V, GND to G, DIN to S on the GP7 header. Use the end the arrows point away from.
  5. Set the power jumper to the battery/VSYS position. Waveshare's note: don't select USB and battery at the same time. USB can stay plugged in for programming; it just shouldn't be the servo supply.
  6. Capacitor polarity. The stripe on the can is the negative leg. Stripe to GND, long leg to 5 V. Backwards, it pops.
  7. Battery polarity. Meter the T-plug pigtail against the pack before plugging in. Red to VIN +.
  8. Servo ranges are bench values. The limits in src/common/settings.py were set with nothing on the horns. Narrow them to what each mechanism allows before mounting. A servo driven into a stop strips itself.
  9. Reset when the head moves? That's the 5 V rail sagging. The cap is the first fix; the code already starts the two head servos one after the other.
